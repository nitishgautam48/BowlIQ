# BowlIQ coaching notes

Cues and drills used in reports come only from this file. Each metric section lists why it matters, cues and drills.
Lines must start with `Why:`, `Cue:` or `Drill:`. Keep the language calm and non-diagnostic.

## stride_length_pct_height
Why: The delivery stride sets up the front-leg block. Too short limits the momentum you carry into front foot contact; too long makes it hard to brace and can increase load on the front leg and back.
Cue: Drive the front knee forward and up at the jump, then land in your natural stride.
Cue: Stay tall through the bound so you land balanced, not reaching.
Drill: Stride markers: put two cones at 75% and 85% of your height from your back-foot mark and land the front foot between them, 3 sets of 6 balls.
Drill: Bound-and-hold: jump into the delivery stride without the ball and freeze at front foot contact, 3 sets of 8.

## front_knee_flexion_ffc_to_release
Why: A front leg that keeps bending after landing absorbs energy instead of passing it up the body to the bowling arm, which costs pace.
Cue: Land on a firm front leg and push up through it to the release.
Cue: Brace the front knee and let the hips ride over the front foot.
Drill: Front-foot block drill: from a two-step approach, land and hold a braced front leg through release, 3 sets of 8 balls.
Drill: Wall-brace holds: front foot against a mark, lean into a braced front leg for 20 seconds, 3 repetitions.

## front_knee_angle_release
Why: A straighter front knee at release is moderately linked to faster ball speed because the body pivots over a firmer lever.
Cue: Finish tall over the front leg at release.
Cue: Push the ground away with the front foot as the arm comes over.
Drill: Standing release drill: stand at front foot contact position and bowl with a braced front leg, 3 sets of 6 balls.

## trunk_forward_flexion_release
Why: The fastest bowlers flex the upper trunk forward strongly up to release, adding speed to the arm.
Cue: Chest drives down towards the target as the arm comes over.
Cue: Pull the non-bowling arm down and in to bring the chest through.
Drill: Knee-height target drill: bowl from a short run aiming to finish with the chest over the front knee, 3 sets of 6 balls.
Drill: Medicine-ball overhead slams over a braced front leg, 3 sets of 8.

## runup_speed
Why: Run-up speed is the strongest single correlate of ball speed: momentum you carry into the crease can be passed on to the ball.
Cue: Build speed gradually and arrive at the crease at the fastest pace you can control.
Cue: Relaxed shoulders and fast feet in the last five strides.
Drill: Marked run-ups: time your last 10 m with a phone and aim for a smooth, slightly quicker approach, 6 runs.
Drill: Accelerations: 3 × 20 m build-ups ending with a delivery-stride bound.

## hip_shoulder_separation_bfc
Why: A large difference between hip and shoulder alignment at back foot contact is part of the mixed-action pattern that has been linked to higher lower-back load.
Cue: Line the hips and shoulders up together at back foot contact.
Cue: Let the shoulders follow the hips rather than twisting against them.
Drill: Alignment walk-throughs: walk into back foot contact and check hips and shoulders point the same way in a mirror or video, 3 sets of 6.

## shoulder_counter_rotation
Why: Shoulder counter-rotation above about 30° between back foot and front foot contact is the classic mixed-action marker, associated with lumbar bone stress injury.
Cue: Keep the shoulders steady between back foot and front foot contact.
Cue: Turn the chest towards the batter together with the hips.
Drill: Slow-motion delivery-stride rehearsals filmed front-on, checking the shoulder line, 3 sets of 5.
Drill: Two-step bowling with a focus on a quiet upper body between the foot contacts, 3 sets of 6 balls.

## trunk_lateral_flexion_release
Why: Side bend at release changes the arm path and is worth monitoring over time with a coach.
Cue: Stay tall through the release.
Drill: Front-on filmed spot bowling to review side bend at release, 2 sets of 6 balls.

## release_height_pct_height
Why: Release height affects bounce and the angle into the pitch; track it over time rather than against a fixed target.
Cue: Reach up tall through the crease.
Drill: High-target release drill: bowl at a target with a tall finish, 3 sets of 6 balls.

## front_knee_angle_ffc
Why: The front knee angle at landing sets up the brace that follows.
Cue: Land with a firm, almost straight front leg.
Drill: Landing holds: bound into front foot contact and hold for two seconds, 3 sets of 6.
